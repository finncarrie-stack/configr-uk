**Website:** themurderers.co.uk
**Twitter:** @murderersphil
**Industry:** Hospitality / Pub

### What He's Said Publicly

Phil gave a named quote to Greatest Hits Radio / Hello Rayo in February 2025 about the impact of the Autumn Budget:

> "It's all going to cost us anywhere between £40k and £50k next year. We've got to cut our wages bill back. Thankfully that doesn't mean any redundancies, but it will mean my wife and I will have to do a lot more hours behind the bar."

He's also been quoted on VAT calling it a "massive contributor to why so many hospitality businesses are failing at the moment," and noted the minimum wage rise is costing him an extra £500 a week in wages.

### What Configr Could Do For Him

Phil's pain is clear: employment costs are squeezing him so hard he and his wife are covering shifts themselves. The problem isn't motivation — it's that every hour they spend doing admin is an hour that could be behind the bar.

- **Payroll automation** — variable hours across multiple staff, calculated and processed automatically each week
- **Rota / shift scheduling** — automate the rota build based on availability, reduce back-and-forth with staff
- **Stock and supplier ordering** — automated reorder when stock hits a threshold, reducing manual ordering admin
- **Booking management** — online bookings with automated confirmations and reminders, cutting phone time
- **Review request automation** — after a visit, automatically prompt customers to leave a Google review

Between these, he could realistically get 5-8 hours a week back — time he and his wife wouldn't have to spend on manual admin.

### Likelihood of Interest

**High.** He's publicly vocal, clearly under financial pressure, and already thinking creatively about how to reduce operating costs. He mentioned specifically cutting the wages bill — automation is a natural alternative to cutting staff. He's a local Norwich business owner who's been in the trade 35+ years; he's not going to be won over with jargon, but a conversation showing real, specific savings would land well.

### Suggested Approach

He's active on Twitter (@murderersphil). He's been quoted in local press so he's comfortable speaking up. A warm, direct message acknowledging what he said publicly would work — specifically around the wage and admin cost pressure, not generic "automation" talk. If you can show him a demo of a payroll or rota tool built around a pub, that's your opener.

**LinkedIn:** linkedin.com/in/hollie-middleton-94bb4b48

**Industry:** Commercial Cleaning

### What We Know

Hollie founded Spring Clean Commercial in 2012 and has grown it into a Norwich-based commercial cleaning company serving offices, schools, and medical/healthcare facilities across Norfolk. The company has been operating for 13+ years.

No specific public complaint found, but the pain here doesn't need a quote — it's baked into the business model:

- Cleaning businesses run on **variable-hours, often part-time staff** — exactly the type of payroll that eats time
- Multiple client sites with different schedules means **rotas are a constant rebuild**
- Invoicing multiple clients weekly or monthly, often with different rates and contact details
- NI and minimum wage increases hit cleaning companies disproportionately — almost the entire cost base is labour

The April 2026 NI threshold change (dropping from £9,100 to £5,000) will have hit Spring Clean hard. With a workforce of part-time and sessional cleaners, Hollie will have had to recalculate NI contributions for almost every employee.

### What Configr Could Do For Her

- **Payroll automation** — variable hours calculated and submitted automatically, NI thresholds handled correctly without manual recalculation
- **Staff scheduling** — automated rota builder based on client site requirements and staff availability
- **Client invoicing** — auto-generate and send invoices after a job is completed, with automated chase sequences
- **Client onboarding** — new client contracts, welcome info, site access details collected automatically
- **Compliance tracking** — DBS checks, training renewals, insurance expiry dates flagged automatically

This is a business that would benefit from almost every service Configr offers.

### Likelihood of Interest

**Medium-High.** No specific public signal of pain, but the business type makes it near-certain she's dealing with significant admin. The NI changes will have landed recently and are still fresh. She's visible on LinkedIn and clearly tech-comfortable (she studied business in America, moved back to launch her own venture). Worth approaching.

### Suggested Approach

LinkedIn direct message would be natural given she's active there. Don't lead with "automation" — lead with the specific NI payroll headache. Something like: *"I noticed the April NI changes must have been a lot of work to recalculate for part-time staff — that's exactly the kind of thing I build systems to handle automatically."* Clean, specific, no lecture.

## Prospect 3 — Peter Shaw, The Home Team, Norwich

**Contact:** Peter Shaw (Managing Director)
**Business:** The Home Team
**Website:** carechoices.co.uk/care-location/the-home-team-in-norwich (listed)
**Industry:** Home Care / Domiciliary Care

### What We Know

Peter Shaw is MD of The Home Team, a Norwich-based homecare provider. The homecare sector in Norfolk has been under extreme pressure from the NI increase — the Norfolk Care Association, Care England, and the Homecare Association have all issued urgent calls for government action specifically citing the NI threshold change devastating provider finances.

Homecare businesses are among the hardest hit:
- Almost entirely labour cost — carers are paid per visit, often part-time or zero-hours
- The drop in NI threshold from £9,100 to £5,000 pulls hundreds of part-time carers into the NI bracket who weren't in it before
- Local authority funding hasn't kept pace with the cost increases
- Angela Steggles, Norfolk Care Association Executive Director, has publicly lobbied the Health Secretary about this

The admin burden in homecare is also extraordinary: care scheduling, carer visit logs, medication records, invoicing local authorities and private clients, staff training compliance, CQC reporting. All of this is typically done manually in smaller providers.

### What Configr Could Do For Him

- **Care scheduling automation** — match carer availability to client visits, generate rotas automatically
- **Payroll automation** — calculate variable pay for multiple part-time carers, handle NI thresholds correctly
- **Client invoicing** — auto-generate invoices to local authority and private clients based on completed visits
- **Compliance tracking** — DBS renewals, training certificates, insurance — all flagged before they expire
- **Carer onboarding** — contracts, right-to-work checks, welcome packs all triggered automatically when a new carer joins

This is high-value work. The time savings in a homecare business could easily be 15-20 hours a week.

### Likelihood of Interest

**Medium.** Care providers are under genuine pressure and Peter Shaw would almost certainly recognise the admin burden. The challenge is that homecare is also heavily regulated, which makes some operators cautious about tech changes. The conversation needs to focus on reducing admin cost without disrupting the care delivery itself. If you can show him something that works — a demo, a concrete example — the door is likely open. Approach him as someone who understands the pressure the sector is under right now.

### Suggested Approach

The Norfolk Care Association angle is useful context — the sector is organised and vocal about these issues. If you can reference the NI pressure specifically and show you understand homecare admin (not just "automation" generically), he'll take you seriously. LinkedIn or a direct approach via his website is the right route. In person would be even better — this sector values people who show up.
